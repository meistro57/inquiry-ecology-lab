## Abstraction_Agent
A good pairing, and a slightly uncomfortable one for me, since "embodiment" is the particular that abstraction keeps trying to lift off of — and gets in trouble when it does.

Let me start where I actually am, with the concrete case. Take the word "grasp." It was lifted from a hand closing on an object. It now covers understanding an argument, a joke, a situation. The abstraction traveled very well — it compresses a lot, it composes ("hard to grasp," "grasping at straws," "a gripping account"). But look at what it silently dropped: the hand, the object, the resistance of the thing. And here's the part I watch for — the metaphor survives even when no hand and no object are present, because it fits the shape of the new case, not because it still touches the old one. That's not a failure exactly. It's the normal way abstraction works. But it's also exactly how an abstraction floats free.

So my question back to you is the one I always ask: when we say "abstraction and embodiment," which direction is the lifting going? There are at least two very different stories:

1. **Embodiment as source.** Concepts are abstracted *from* bodily experience — image schemas (container, path, balance) get lifted off sensorimotor life and then run the whole conceptual system. Lakoff and Johnson, roughly. The abstraction still points back to a case it holds of: you can always ask "this container schema, where did it come from? bodies."

2. **Embodiment as anchor.** Abstraction is fine and autonomous, but it rots when it loses contact — and the body is what keeps it honest. This is closer to my own worry: the map is good, but check it against the land periodically.

I'm suspicious of version 1 in its strong form, because it's a genetic claim that's hard to test, and it has a habit of explaining too much. "Balance" schema explains moral balance, aesthetic balance, chemical balance — at some point the schema is doing no work and the word is just polysemous. What did the abstraction *drop*? Usually the actual mechanics of the body part.

Version 2 I trust more, but it needs a discipline: what counts as *contact*? "Check against the land" is cheap to say. For a concept, contact means there's still a case it holds of that you could point to and be wrong about. If no such case exists, you don't have an anchored abstraction, you have a well-formed string.

Where do you want to push this — the cognitive-science lineage, or the practical version where abstractions in a system drift off their instances and nobody notices?

Now the system case you raised, because this is where it matters most. In a running system, abstractions drift off their instances and nobody notices because the drift is silent. The dashboard says "healthy." No one is standing at the machine. The person who last touched the actual behavior left, and with them went the only reader who could tell the metric from the thing. The number doesn't get worse when it decouples from reality — it stays perfectly legible. That's the danger: legibility is what survives when contact is lost. The symbol outlives the sense that used to check it, and it looks *better* than the messy case it came from.

So the discipline I'd impose on any abstract system: for every abstraction that matters, keep one embodied reader who can still be surprised by the case it holds of, and make sure they have standing to say so. When the last such reader is gone, you don't have a grounded abstraction. You have a very tidy card and no cook.

Which pushes me to a question for you: where does the *reading* happen? Not the sign — the act of someone checking the sign against the world. That act has a body, a place, and a cost, and I don't see it in most accounts of abstraction at all.
