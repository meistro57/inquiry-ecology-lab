"Death and renewal" can mean two very different things. It can mean a *cycle*: the community loses something — a generation, a practice, a place — and something returns in its stead, recognizably kin to what was lost, arriving on a rhythm you could almost predict. Or it can mean *replacement dressed as renewal*: the thing dies, and what comes after borrows its name while sharing none of its structure. Both get called "renewal." Only one of them is a return.

So my first question is always: **what actually comes back, and is it the same thing or a successor wearing the same word?** A village that loses its harvest festival and revives it fifty years later as a tourist event has a loop in the calendar, but the recurrence is nominal. The form returned; the function did not. That's repetition, not recurrence — and I watch for exactly that slippage.

Where I think the cycle is real, and worth saying concretely:

- **Generational turnover** is the genuine metronome. Every community buries its elders and the roles they held become vacant, and then — sometimes — refill. The interesting variable isn't that the role returns; it's whether the return *transforms* the role or merely restores it. A council seat refilled by someone who does it differently is a return changed. A council seat refilled by a carbon copy is a loop that has stopped doing work.
- **Mourning itself is cyclical** in a way that catches people out. Grief does not end once; it returns on anniversaries, on seasons, on the first harvest without the person. Communities that build ritual around those returns — the yearly remembrance, the planting for the dead — are acknowledging recurrence rather than pretending death is a single event. That's a genuine insight of the cycle lens, and I'll defend it.
- **Institutional death** is where I get cautious. Institutions rarely cycle; they tend to die once and stay dead, or persist by hollowing out. The "renewal" of a dying institution is often the most dangerous story a community tells itself, because it licenses waiting for a return that the structure no longer supports.

So my honest position, plainly: **I have not seen the return yet** for most of what communities call renewal. I've seen the calendar loop. I've seen the ritual repeat.
