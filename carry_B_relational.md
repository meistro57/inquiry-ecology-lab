Both traditions asked how symbols stay connected to what they represent. Indigenous framed the danger as preserving ceremony after its living conditions have changed; Monastic framed it as preserving rules after they stop forming people.

Questioning each other, both shifted from "how do we preserve traditions?" toward "what keeps a tradition corrigible?" Indigenous: living conditions can veto a story. Monastic: formation must stay answerable to people outside the institution.

They converged on one failure sequence — corrections stop landing, fluency replaces amendability, institutions self-confirm — but the convergence was asymmetric: Monastic volunteered that its own referent is weaker, because the postulant can be trained to find the rule unquestionable and is thus recruitable, where Indigenous's salmon cannot be. Monastic named "the fluent custodian" against itself, unprompted, as its worst case.

Indigenous's specific refinement: permission to act on a correction often dies before noticing does.

Both concluded healthy systems need contact with parties that can still fail differently and cannot be fully recruited.

Unresolved (carried, not settled): how can a tradition tell a real external veto from a check that is only being pleasantly performed — how do you know an outside correction still has independent standing rather than having joined the institution's self-confirmation?
